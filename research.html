Research

Intelligent Wireless Networks Under Resource Constraints

My research focuses on intelligent wireless sensor and IoT systems, particularly under energy, communication and resource constraints. Across different projects, I have worked on learning-based approaches for network optimization, energy management, intrusion detection and distributed decision making, using methods from reinforcement learning, federated learning and graph-based machine learning. A consistent theme in this work is the consideration of learning performance together with system-level factors such as communication overhead, energy consumption, scalability and operational constraints. Beyond wireless and IoT systems, I have also worked on machine-learning applications involving Edge-AI sensing and transformer-based sequence modeling.

Wireless Sensor Networks · Energy Optimization · Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning

Spatially-Constrained Event-Driven Hierarchical Deep Reinforcement Learning (SCED-HRL)

SCED-HRL is my B.Sc. thesis framework for energy-aware WSN control. It separates network-level strategic adaptation from node-level tactical decisions and adds spatial constraints so that learning does not improve one part of the network by prematurely exhausting another.

Core design: an event-driven PPO strategic layer activates only when an adaptive instability score requires intervention; a spatial budgeting layer distributes region-wise energy constraints; DDQN tactical agents make local routing/operation decisions under action masking and feasibility guards.
  • Simulation uses a 50-node WSN with traffic derived from the Intel Berkeley sensor dataset plus synthetic traffic conditions.
  • Evaluation covers network lifetime, energy balance, packet delivery and robustness under multiple traffic patterns.
  • Reported results in the submitted manuscript include 2,143-round LND, 94.2% PDR and energy CV 0.17.
  • Relative to the strongest fixed-spatial HRL baseline, the study reports 13.8% longer lifetime; the event-trigger mechanism reduces strategic invocations by 83%, and spatial budgeting improves energy fairness by 26%.

Federated Learning · Intrusion Detection · Temporal GNNs · WSN Communication Cost

THRIFT: Selective Graph-Teacher Knowledge Transfer for Federated Intrusion Detection

THRIFT studies whether a lightweight federated WSN detector can selectively obtain topology-aware teacher knowledge without continuously retransmitting expensive information. Gateways retain local training records while compact telemetry supports graph reconstruction and a frozen temporal-GNN teacher on the server.

System idea: compressed teacher targets are cached at clients and refreshed only when a server-side signal gate opens, with a cooldown preventing back-to-back transfers. Communication accounting includes model traffic, teacher targets, summaries and graph telemetry.
  • Across 98 telemetry messages, reconstructed node sets, timing and topology matched the reference audit, with unchanged teacher F1 at reported precision.
  • Five diagnostic runs report 12.54 MB mean serialized communication for THRIFT versus 14.42 MB periodic and 14.53 MB always-on transfer.
  • The reported communication reductions are 13.01% versus periodic and 13.69% versus always-on transfer.
  • An OMNeT++ packet-radio replay preserves the same ranking, with modeled radio energy about 13% lower than the two controls.
